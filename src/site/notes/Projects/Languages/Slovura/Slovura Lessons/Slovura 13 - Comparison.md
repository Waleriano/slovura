# [[Projects/Languages/Slovura/Slovura Lessons/Slovura 13 - Comparison\|Slovura 13 - Comparison]]

[[Projects/Languages/Slovura/Slovura\|Slovura]] compares qualities using the [[Projects/Languages/Slovura/Slovura Lessons/Slovura 10 - Whole-Word Modifiers\|whole-word modifiers]] `po` and `ti`.

- [[Projects/Languages/Slovura/Slovura Roots/po\|po]] — more
- [[Projects/Languages/Slovura/Slovura Roots/ti\|ti]] — all

Because they modify the complete word before them, they work with both simple words and [[Projects/Languages/Slovura/Slovura Lessons/Slovura 04 - Compound Words\|compounds]].

## Comparative: `-po`

Add `po` to express **more** of a quality:

> **sta** — big
>
> **stapo** — `(sta) po` — **bigger**

The same pattern can be used with other qualities when comparison makes sense.

## Superlative: `-poti`

Add `ti` after the comparative:

> **stapoti**
>
> `((sta) po) ti`
>
> **biggest**

`-poti` is not a separate indivisible ending. It is two stacked whole-word modifiers:

> **word + po + ti**

First `po` forms the comparative. Then `ti` applies to that complete comparative form.

## `ti` Without `po`

`ti` also works independently. As a whole-word modifier, it means **all members or the entirety of the preceding concept**.

> **ka** — person
>
> **kati** — `(ka) ti` — **all people / everyone**

Compare:

> **kate** — multiple people
>
> **kati** — all people / everyone

So `ti` is not inherently a superlative marker. The superlative emerges from combining `po` and `ti`.

## Comparison with Compounds

Comparison applies after the compound has been constructed:

> **ABCpo** → `(ABC) po` → more of `ABC`
>
> **ABCpoti** → `((ABC) po) ti` → most of `ABC`

## The Pattern

| Form | Structure | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `X` | `X` | X |
| `Xpo` | `(X) po` | more X |
| `Xpoti` | `((X) po) ti` | most X |
| `Xte` | `(X) te` | multiple X |
| `Xti` | `(X) ti` | all X |

> **sta** → big
>
> **stapo** → bigger
>
> **stapoti** → biggest

## Remember

> [!summary]
> - `-po` forms a comparative: `sta` → `stapo`.
> - Adding `-ti` to the comparative forms a superlative: `stapo` → `stapoti`.
> - `-poti` is `-po` + `-ti`, not a separate ending.
> - `po` and `ti` work with compounds because they have whole-word scope.
> - `ti` can also stand alone as a whole-word modifier meaning all or entirety.
> - `kate` means multiple people; `kati` means all people.
