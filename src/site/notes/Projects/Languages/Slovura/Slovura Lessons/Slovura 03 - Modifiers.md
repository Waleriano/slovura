# [[Projects/Languages/Slovura/Slovura Lessons/Slovura 03 - Modifiers\|Slovura 03 - Modifiers]]

A [[Projects/Languages/Slovura/Slovura\|Slovura]] [[Projects/Languages/Slovura/Slovura Lessons/Slovura 01 - Slovura Roots\|root]] often expresses a broad concept. To make that concept more specific, another word can be placed after it as a **modifier**.

Slovura uses **head-first modification**.

> [!note] Basic modifier structure
> **head + modifier**
>
> The first word tells you what the thing fundamentally is. The following word describes it.

## Head and Modifier

Consider:

> **[[Projects/Languages/Slovura/Slovura Roots/ka\|ka]] [[Projects/Languages/Slovura/Slovura Roots/sta\|sta]]**

`ka` means **person**, while `sta` includes meanings such as **big** or **tall**.

Because the head comes first, `ka` tells us that we are talking about a person. `sta` then tells us something about that person.

> **ka sta**
>
> person + big
>
> **a big person / a tall person**

The exact English translation depends on context. The important structure is:

> **a `ka` that is `sta`**

The same principle works with other heads:

> **[[Projects/Languages/Slovura/Slovura Roots/ma\|ma]] [[Projects/Languages/Slovura/Slovura Roots/sta\|sta]]**
>
> land + big
>
> **a large land**

Here `ma` is the head and `sta` describes it.

## More Than One Modifier

A head can be followed by more than one modifier.

> [!note] Multiple modifiers
> **head + modifier + modifier + ...**

The first word remains the head. The words after it add information and make the phrase more specific.

A phrase therefore begins with the broad concept and then narrows it by adding descriptions.

## Meaning Comes From Context

Because Slovura roots represent broad concepts, modifiers do not always have one fixed English translation.

For example, `sta` can be understood as **big**, **tall**, **long**, **important**, or another related idea depending on what it modifies and the surrounding context.

With `ka`, **tall** may often be the natural interpretation:

> `ka sta` → **a tall person**

With `ma`, **large** is more natural:

> `ma sta` → **a large land**

The Slovura structure itself remains the same: **head first, description after it**.

## Remember

> [!summary]
> - Slovura uses **head-first modification**.
> - The first word is the **head**.
> - Words after the head **modify** or describe it.
> - The best English translation of a modifier depends on context.
