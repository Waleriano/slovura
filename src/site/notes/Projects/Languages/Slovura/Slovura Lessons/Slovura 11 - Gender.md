# [[Projects/Languages/Slovura/Slovura Lessons/Slovura 11 - Gender\|Slovura 11 - Gender]]

[[Projects/Languages/Slovura/Slovura\|Slovura]] does not require gender to be specified.

When gender is relevant, it can be added directly to a word using one of three endings:

- `-na` — feminine
- `-no` — masculine
- `-n` — gender-neutral

These are [[Projects/Languages/Slovura/Slovura Lessons/Slovura 10 - Whole-Word Modifiers\|whole-word modifiers]].

## Gender Is Optional

A word without a gender ending does not imply any particular gender.

For example:

> **le**
>
> third-person pronoun without specified gender

Gender can be added when you want to express it:

> **lena**
>
> **[[Projects/Languages/Slovura/Slovura Roots/le\|le]] + [[Projects/Languages/Slovura/Slovura Roots/na\|na]]**
>
> **she**

> **leno**
>
> **[[Projects/Languages/Slovura/Slovura Roots/le\|le]] + [[Projects/Languages/Slovura/Slovura Roots/no\|no]]**
>
> **he**

> **len**
>
> **le + n**
>
> **gender-neutral third-person pronoun**

The unmarked `le` and gender-neutral `len` are different: `le` leaves gender unspecified, while `len` explicitly expresses a gender-neutral form.

## Gender in Compound Words

Gender can be added after an already constructed concept:

> **[[Projects/Languages/Slovura/Slovura Words/kasapa\|kasapa]]**
>
> **[[Projects/Languages/Slovura/Slovura Roots/ka\|ka]] + [[Projects/Languages/Slovura/Slovura Roots/sa\|sa]] + [[Projects/Languages/Slovura/Slovura Roots/pa\|pa]]**
>
> person + same + parent
>
> **sibling**

> **kasapana** → `(kasapa) na` → **sister**
>
> **kasapano** → `(kasapa) no` → **brother**

The base `kasapa` remains sibling without specifying gender.

## Masculine and Feminine Are Broad

`na` and `no` are broader than biological sex. They express **feminine** and **masculine** forms or interpretations and can be used wherever that distinction is meaningful.

## Remember

> [!summary]
> - Gender is optional.
> - `-na` is feminine, `-no` masculine, and `-n` gender-neutral.
> - An unmarked word leaves gender unspecified.
> - `le`, `lena`, `leno`, and `len` therefore express different gender information.
> - Gender endings apply to the complete word before them.
