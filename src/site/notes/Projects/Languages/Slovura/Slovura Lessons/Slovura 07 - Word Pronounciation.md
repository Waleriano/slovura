# [[Projects/Languages/Slovura/Slovura Lessons/Slovura 07 - Word Pronounciation\|Slovura 07 - Word Pronounciation]]

By this point, you have already seen Slovura roots used both as separate words and joined together into [[Projects/Languages/Slovura/Slovura Lessons/Slovura 04 - Compound Words\|compound words]]. Pronunciation follows a simple rule that helps distinguish between the two.

## Stress

In Slovura, **stress always falls on the first syllable of a word**.

Since every Slovura root consists of one syllable, a root used as a separate word is stressed on that syllable.

When several roots are joined into a compound, however, they form **one word**. The compound therefore has only one primary stress: on its first syllable.

> [!note] Slovura stress
> **The first syllable of every word is stressed.**
>
> Separate words are each stressed individually. A compound word is stressed only on its first syllable.

## Compounds and Separate Words

This stress pattern makes it possible to hear the difference between a compound and the same sequence of roots spoken as separate words.

For example:

> [!example] One compound
> **KA**-kla-ma
>
> `kaklama`
>
> One word, so only its first syllable is stressed.

Compare it with:

> [!example] Three separate words
> **KA KLA MA**
>
> `ka kla ma`
>
> Three words, so each word begins with its own stress.

The sequence of roots is the same:

> `ka` + `kla` + `ma`

What changes is how the speaker groups them into words.

This is important because [[Projects/Languages/Slovura/Slovura Lessons/Slovura 04 - Compound Words\|compounding is optional]]. A speaker can choose to combine roots into one concept or leave them as separate words. Stress makes that distinction audible in speech.

## Root Boundaries

Slovura roots end in vowels. When roots are combined into a compound, their pronunciation does not change and no additional sound needs to be inserted between them.

For example:

> `ka` + `kla` + `ma` → `kaklama`

The individual roots remain recognizable inside the compound.

Together with first-syllable stress, this gives Slovura a simple distinction:

- **one written word → one initial stress**
- **several written words → each word has its own initial stress**

## Why Stress Matters

In many languages, stress is primarily a feature of pronunciation. In Slovura it also helps communicate **word boundaries**.

Compare:

> **KA**-kla-ma — one compound concept
>
> **KA KLA MA** — three separate words

This allows Slovura to keep its roots short and to join them directly while still providing an audible clue to whether the speaker intends them as one compound or as separate words.

## Remember

> [!summary]
> - Every Slovura word is stressed on its **first syllable**.
> - A one-syllable root used by itself is therefore stressed.
> - A compound has **one primary stress**, on its first root.
> - Separate words each receive their own stress.
> - Roots keep the same pronunciation when they are joined into compounds.
> - Stress helps listeners distinguish compounds from sequences of separate words.
