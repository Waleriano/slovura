# [[Projects/Languages/Slovura/Slovura Lessons/Slovura 10 - Whole-Word Modifiers\|Slovura 10 - Whole-Word Modifiers]]

Most [[Projects/Languages/Slovura/Slovura Lessons/Slovura 04 - Compound Words\|compound words]] follow the grouping rules in [[Projects/Languages/Slovura/Slovura Lessons/Slovura 09 - Modifier Grouping\|Modifier Grouping]]. Some [[Projects/Languages/Slovura/Slovura Lessons/Slovura 01 - Slovura Roots\|roots]] behave differently at the **end of a word**: they modify the **entire word constructed before them**.

These are **whole-word modifiers**.

## Whole-Word Scope

A normal compound follows normal compound grouping. A final whole-word modifier `X` instead takes scope over everything before it:

> **ABCX** → `(ABC) X`

Whole-word modifiers can stack:

> **ABCXY** → `((ABC) X) Y`

Each modifier applies to the complete word formed before it.

Their special property here is their **scope**. At the end of a word, they apply to the whole preceding concept.

## `pi` Can Override the Grouping

[[Projects/Languages/Slovura/Slovura Roots/pi\|pi]] can explicitly change this scope.

> **ABCX** → `(ABC) X`
>
> **ABpiCX** → `(A B) (C X)`

The explicit grouping created by `pi` takes priority.

## Remember

> [!summary]
> - Whole-word modifiers apply to the complete word before them.
> - `ABCX` becomes `(ABC) X`.
> - They can stack: `ABCXY` becomes `((ABC) X) Y`.
> - Their individual uses are introduced in the following lessons.
> - `pi` can override their normal scope.
