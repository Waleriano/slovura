# [[Projects/Languages/Slovura/Slovura Lessons/Slovura 07 - Word Stress\|Slovura 07 - Word Stress]]

[[Projects/Languages/Slovura/Slovura\|Slovura]] has one simple stress rule:

> [!note] Word stress
> **Stress always falls on the first syllable of a word.**

Since every basic [[Projects/Languages/Slovura/Slovura Lessons/Slovura 01 - Slovura Roots\|root]] has one syllable, a root used as a separate word is stressed on that syllable.

A [[Projects/Languages/Slovura/Slovura Lessons/Slovura 04 - Compound Words\|compound]] is a single word, so only its first syllable receives the primary stress.

## Stress and Word Boundaries

Stress helps distinguish the [[Projects/Languages/Slovura/Slovura Lessons/Slovura 05 - Phrases vs Compounds\|phrases and compounds]] introduced earlier.

Compare:

> **KA STA**
>
> `ka sta`
>
> two words, so each word is stressed

with:

> **KA**-sta
>
> `kasta`
>
> one word, so only the first syllable is stressed

The same applies to longer compounds:

> **KA KLA MA** — `ka kla ma` — three separate words
>
> **KA**-kla-ma — `kaklama` — one compound word

The roots themselves do not change. Stress simply provides an audible indication of how they are grouped into words.

## Remember

> [!summary]
> - Stress always falls on the **first syllable of a word**.
> - Separate words are each stressed.
> - A compound has one primary stress, on its first syllable.
> - Stress helps make word boundaries audible.
