# [[Projects/Languages/Slovura/Slovura Lessons/Slovura 10 - Compounding Exceptions\|Slovura 10 - Compounding Exceptions]]

Most [[Projects/Languages/Slovura/Slovura Lessons/Slovura 04 - Compound Words\|compound words]] follow the normal grouping rules introduced in [[Projects/Languages/Slovura/Slovura Lessons/Slovura 09 - Modifier Grouping\|modifier grouping]].

Some [[Projects/Languages/Slovura/Slovura Lessons/Slovura 01 - Slovura Roots\|roots]], however, can have special behaviour in particular positions.

This lesson collects exceptions to the normal compounding rules.

## Final `na` and `no`

The roots [[Projects/Languages/Slovura/Slovura Roots/na\|na]] and [[Projects/Languages/Slovura/Slovura Roots/no\|no]] are an exception when they occur at the **end of a compound word**.

Instead of following the normal compound grouping, a final `na` or `no` modifies the **entire compound before it**.

> [!note] Final `na` or `no`
> **ABCna** → `(ABC) na`
>
> **ABCno** → `(ABC) no`

This makes it possible to construct a concept first and then apply `na` or `no` to that concept as a whole.

## Example: Siblings

Consider:

> **kasapa**
>
> **[[Projects/Languages/Slovura/Slovura Roots/ka\|ka]] + [[Projects/Languages/Slovura/Slovura Roots/sa\|sa]] + [[Projects/Languages/Slovura/Slovura Roots/pa\|pa]]**
>
> person + same + parent
>
> **sibling**

The compound describes a person associated with the same parent.

Adding `no` at the end does not make it part of the normal internal grouping. Instead, it modifies `kasapa` as a whole:

> [!example] Brother
> **kasapano**
>
> `(kasapa) no`
>
> sibling + male
>
> **brother**

Likewise:

> [!example] Sister
> **kasapana**
>
> `(kasapa) na`
>
> sibling + female
>
> **sister**

The concepts **sibling**, **brother**, and **sister** can therefore be built from the same underlying structure.

## Overriding the Exception with `pi`

The grouping marker [[Projects/Languages/Slovura/Slovura Roots/pi\|pi]] can override this special behaviour.

Normally, a final `na` or `no` takes scope over the whole compound before it:

> **ABCno**
>
> `(ABC) no`

If `pi` explicitly groups the final root with only part of the compound, that grouping takes priority:

> **ABpiCno**
>
> `(A B) (C no)`

Here `no` belongs to the grouped concept `C no` instead of modifying the entire preceding compound.

This gives `pi` an additional role in compounds: it can make the intended structure explicit when a special compounding rule would otherwise produce a different grouping.

## Why Have Exceptions?

The normal compound rules should handle most words. Exceptions are useful when a particular root has a productive role that would otherwise require awkward or ambiguous constructions.

They should therefore be treated as **specific rules**, not as a reason to guess the structure of compounds freely.

Unless a documented exception applies, use the normal compound grouping from [[Projects/Languages/Slovura/Slovura Lessons/Slovura 09 - Modifier Grouping\|Modifier Grouping]].

## Remember

> [!summary]
> - Compounds normally follow [[Projects/Languages/Slovura/Slovura\|Slovura]]'s standard compound-grouping rules.
> - Final [[Projects/Languages/Slovura/Slovura Roots/na\|na]] or [[Projects/Languages/Slovura/Slovura Roots/no\|no]] modifies the entire compound before it.
> - [[Projects/Languages/Slovura/Slovura Roots/pi\|pi]] can override the exception by explicitly changing the grouping.
